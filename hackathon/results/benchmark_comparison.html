Wilson loop step: speed-up over PythTB's berry_phase

Wilson loop only, on the same states: 6 configs at W = 4 (team convention). PythTB loops over lines with one SVD each; ours does every line of every config in one batch. Measured by profile_wilson.py.

Newton-Schulz beats the batched SVD from 6×6 up, and the gain grows with matrix size. At 3×3 (18×18 overlaps) NS in double is no faster than PythTB.

Where the batched Wilson loop spends its time (double)

Stages of wilson.wannier_centres. Newton-Schulz runs 12 fixed steps, but on these overlaps it has converged by step 3–4 (residual ≤ 3e-15 in double; it reaches its rounding floor, ~1e-6, by step 2–3 in single).

Second-biggest cost at 8×8: eigvals of the Wilson matrices (33%). Smallest overlap singular value in these runs: 0.96–0.98, so no SVD fallbacks were needed.

Physics check: fraction of configurations that are topological

Fraction with Z2 = 1 against disorder strength, all runs on the tutorial parameters (delta = 1, soc = 0.3, rashba = 0.25). W in Orth et al.'s convention, on-site energies uniform in [−W, W] (the team code's W is twice this).

Only 3–6 configurations per point, so these are rough. The team runs count only configurations that pass the reliability checks (z2_reliable); the sample-sim runs have no such filter. These are small supercells on the tutorial parameters, not the paper's λν = 1.45–1.85 TAI parameters.

Which ops run on the Metal GPU

TensorFlow 2.18.1 + tensorflow-metal 1.2.0, soft placement off.

PythTB's TensorFlow path calls complex128 eigh, so on a Mac it runs entirely on the CPU.

eigh variants

Clean supercell H(k) stack, 24×24 mesh (576 matrices). Seconds.

NumPy's batched eigh uses one core. Eigenvectors cost ~70% of eigh.

PyTorch CPU threading

eigh: 6×6, 384 matrices of 144×144. Newton-Schulz and SVD: 6×6, 384 overlaps of 72×72 (12 NS steps).

Thread count makes no difference for any of these batched ops on this Mac, so the batched engine runs on one core while PythTB-parallel uses twelve.

float32 matmul: NumPy vs Metal

Batched 576 × M × M, the one op Metal runs. Milliseconds, including the copy back.

Newton-Schulz: stopping early

Newton-Schulz plus convergence check on the real overlaps (6 configs, W = 4), CPU. The 12-step and 4-step answers agree with SVD equally well.

Projected effect of stopping at 4 steps (component swap, not measured end to end): Wilson step at 6×6 double 0.385 → ~0.21 s, i.e. ~2.8× faster than PythTB instead of 1.5×. A convergence check every step or two, with 12 as the cap, would keep the safety margin near gap closings.
