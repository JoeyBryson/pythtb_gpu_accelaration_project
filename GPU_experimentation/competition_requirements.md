# Scientific Parallelism Hackathon

## Challenge

- Choose a scientifically relevant computational problem from research, an existing codebase, or another field.
- Redesign its solution for massively parallel hardware. Consider how work is divided, how data moves, and which operations can run concurrently.
- Build the solution and benchmark it against a meaningful baseline. Measure and explain its performance.
- Show what scientific investigation the approach enables, such as more experiments, higher resolution, improved uncertainty estimates, or a new capability.

Possible approaches include exposing independent computations, reformulating algorithms, changing data representations, or making previously impractical computations feasible.

## Judging criteria

Strong submissions demonstrate some combination of:

- **Correctness and assumptions:** Preserve intended behaviour, or clearly state and justify assumptions introduced to make the problem tractable.
- **Fundamental reshaping:** Creatively restructure the problem, algorithm, or data flow to expose parallelism.
- **Meaningful performance improvement:** Demonstrate a clear speedup against a credible, relevant baseline and explain the measurements.
- **Scientific value:** Provide useful results, sound methods, and convincing technical evidence. Well-explained negative results are also valuable.

## Resources

GPU access, AI/API credits, and support from judges and mentors are provided.
